1997–2025 · New Zealand · preprint

From Lagging to Leading

Twenty-nine years of half-hourly regulatory metering at New Zealand’s grid exit points. It measures the reactive character of a whole power system, and shows it crossing from lagging to leading around 2016.

Most of the drift is on the demand side, about 80% of it. What sits behind that looks like standing capacitance building up behind consumer connections, roughly 230 VAr per connection over 2013–2025. I think the mandated EMC filter inside every switched-mode power supply is the likely mechanism, but that is a hypothesis and it is labelled as one.

Both inputs are records any system operator already holds, so the measurement should travel. The figures here are live rather than printed — hover any chart for its values, or drag the capacitance assumption in Fig. 3.

2026 · Rasuwa, Nepal · ongoing

The Trishuli Investigation

An independent physics analysis of the Langtang Lirung collapse of 26 August 2026, and the flood it sent down the Lhende Khola, the Bhote Koshi and the Trishuli. Reported elsewhere as the Bhote Koshi flood. As at 4 September, 1,259 people were confirmed dead in Nepal and 5,083 missing.

One narrow question: where did the water actually come from? I think it was largely already in the river. Melting ice is expensive and falling is cheap, so a ~1,200 m fall cannot supply the ~20 Mm³ officially called excess. Keep only the model runs that reproduce every observable, and the collapse itself comes out at 14–34 Mm³.

Still going, so treat the numbers above as working numbers rather than results. That 14–34 Mm³ range rests on ten surviving runs and a deposition cap I now think is too tight, so it will move. Five findings retracted in the first week, three more corrected after reading the published pages cold. All of them still up, with the reasons.
